{# Inline icon set — the RSVP ticket family, drawn to match the FontAwesome `fa-slab-duo fa-regular fa-ticket` reference. Hand-drawn on a 24×20 grid rather than pulled from an icon font: the app vendors every asset (no CDN, no build step — see .claude/deployment.md), a webfont for four glyphs is a poor trade, and hand paths sidestep icon-font licensing/attribution entirely. Proportions follow the reference and only the reference — it is a good deal squarer than a real-world ticket, ~1.25:1, with generous corners and a slab outline about a tenth of the height. ── The edge is what makes it a ticket ──────────────────────────────────── TWO bites per end, not one: flat, notch, longer flat, notch, flat. A single central notch reads as a waist — the silhouette pinches in the middle and the thing looks like a spool. Two shallower ones read as perforations, which is the whole visual argument that this is a ticket. The middle flat is deliberately longer than the two end flats, as in the reference. Notch depth is bounded by the outline: ink grows outward from the path, so a bite much shallower than the outline is half-swallowed and stops registering. That is why the rings below are lighter than a true slab — the silhouette matters more than the weight. ── How the layering works ──────────────────────────────────────────────── The wrapper (`.rsvp-flag`) supplies one hue in two shades: `color` is the light tint the linework is drawn in, `--tk-fill` the dark one the ticket's BODY is filled with. The dark is a fill on the `tk-body` path, not a background behind the icon, so it stops exactly at the ticket's edge instead of boxing it in. (It has to arrive as a custom property read by a CSS rule: `var()` is inert inside an SVG presentation attribute.) The silhouette is stroked THREE times over the same path, widest first — white, colour, black — and the body fill is drawn LAST. Because a centred stroke straddles the path, the fill covers the inner half of all three rings, and what survives reads outward from the body as: black hairline, coloured line, white hairline. That is the only way to get an asymmetric edge out of SVG, which has no "inner border" the way CSS does and no way to offset a stroke to one side of its path. The marks printed on each ticket (tick / clock / exclamation) get the simpler two-pass treatment — a fatter black copy, then the coloured one — since they are open strokes with no inside to speak of. This is also the right inversion of the reference: there the duotone mark is a dark outline filled with the ticket's own light body colour, so on our dark body the equivalent is a light stroke and no fill at all.
